Federated learning simulation

Predicting diabetic risk across hospitals without moving patient records

Each simulated hospital trains a logistic regression on its own patients. Only the model weights travel to a coordinator, which averages them (FedAvg) into one shared model. Change any setting and the whole simulation reruns in your browser.

Patients are synthetic, generated from the eight Pima diabetes features with a different case mix at every site. Not for clinical use.

What each hospital gets from joining

Single-hospital AUC comes from a model trained only on that site's patients. The update size is how far the site moved the shared weights at the round selected above.

How much does privacy noise cost?

Each site's update is clipped to a norm of 1 and Gaussian noise scaled by σ is added to the aggregate. Every bar is the final AUC averaged over three runs at your current settings.

One round, step by step

  1. STEP 1BroadcastThe coordinator sends the current shared weights to every hospital.
  2. STEP 2Train locallyEach site runs a few epochs of mini-batch gradient descent on its own patients.
  3. STEP 3Protect and uploadEach site sends only the change in its nine weights, clipped and, if σ is above zero, noised at aggregation.
  4. STEP 4AverageThe coordinator weights each update by site size and applies the result. Repeat.
The privacy noise here shows the accuracy cost of clipping and noising updates. It is a teaching illustration with no formal (ε, δ) accounting, so do not read it as a privacy guarantee. Real deployments add secure aggregation, audited privacy budgets and clinical validation on real records.
